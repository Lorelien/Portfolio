Lumière Cinema Mechelen — App Design

For this school project, I designed a mobile app for city cinema Lumière Cinema in Mechelen. The assignment combined user research, a benchmark based on the 21 UX Laws and an interactive interface in Figma.

After joint wireframes, I individually developed the high-fidelity app designs, including Figma variables, ticket flows and a customisable dark mode.

The assignment

We analysed the existing Lumière website using the 21 UX Laws and then created mobile-first wireframes as a group. After that, each group member individually developed a high-fidelity app design.

The app had to include two experiences: one for a new user and one for someone who has been using the app for at least a year. Required features included search, favourites, purchased tickets, notifications, contact and various feedback pop-ups.

In addition, I worked with Figma variables, among other things for profile photos, ticket selection and a customisable dark mode.

Benchmark of the existing website

Our group benchmarked the existing Lumière website using the 21 UX Laws, including Fitts’ Law, Hick’s Law and the principle of proximity.

The site had a strong visual identity and a clear focus on the film experience, but some interactions were less intuitive.

Certain information was also difficult to find and the ticket flow could be simpler. These insights formed the basis for our wireframes.

User stories and flowchart (group phase)

To make user needs concrete, we created user stories as a group. These helped us determine which screens, information and actions should be prioritised in the app.

Based on these user stories, we created a flowchart with the main routes: choosing a film, selecting a screening, buying tickets, logging in, viewing favourites and managing settings.

The flowchart gave us one shared structure before we started the wireframes. It made clear where there are knots in the flow, where users might get stuck and which screens should logically follow each other.

From group wireframes to individual design

After the benchmark, we created group wireframes for the homepage, registration, login, films, settings, FAQ, contact, account management and the ticket flow, among others. I worked out the “Now in cinemas” and “Coming soon” screens.

After that, I individually translated the shared structure into high-fidelity designs. I refined the visual hierarchy, interactions and micro-interactions for both new and experienced users.

Features and interactions

The app includes a favourites list, a search function with instant results, an overview of purchased tickets and a notifications page. This allows users to save films, find them again and quickly consult their reservations.

I also designed feedback pop-ups for no internet connection, a successfully submitted contact form and leaving the ticket flow. These messages give the user clarity and help prevent unintended actions.

Working with variables

Variables were an important part of the assignment. In account management, users can change their profile photo via a variable and immediately see the result.

For ticket selection, I linked plus and minus buttons to the number of tickets and the total price. This way, the price adjusts immediately when a user adds or removes tickets.

Result

The final result is an interactive mobile app for Lumière Cinema, with a consistent visual identity, clear navigation and attention to micro-interactions. The app includes all required features, from searching for films and saving favourites to managing tickets and adjusting settings.

I designed two user experiences: an experienced user gets quick access to personal content, while a new user receives clear guidance. For that new user, I created empty states for notifications, favourites and purchased tickets, with animated icons that invite users to explore the features.
